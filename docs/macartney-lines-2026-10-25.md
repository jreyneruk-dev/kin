> **Update, 2026-10-26:** the land evidence in §2 and §5 has been superseded.

John's request: *"We did an exercise to work forward from the 1st Earl Macartney and it
seems like there are multiple reasons why there is not a link to my tree. Please work
further back in time on my tree to the same era, so that I will be able to fully compare."*

This report answers it in both directions: it pushes our line back as far as the surviving
records allow, and it puts the two lines side by side at the same dates. The machine-readable
version of the comparison is `src/lib/macartney-lines.ts`; the page is `/compare`.

## Verdict

**The connection is unproved in the sources checked; that is not proof the families were unrelated.**
John confirms that Sheila McCartney was Kathleen S Macartney; I have therefore treated that
identity as family-confirmed, not merely a guess from the initial. Back through the Dublin line,
the earlier marriage-register readings identify William Henry’s father as Richard Henry and
Richard’s father as Henry Macartney, farmer. For the older Antrim period, the accessible Connor
Griffith’s Valuation transcription lists a separate Henry McCartney in Cromkill in 1861–62.
Nothing found joins that tenant to Richard’s father or to the Lissanoure/Earl line.

What this research establishes or qualifies:

- **John confirms Kathleen S Macartney was Sheila McCartney.** This is family-confirmed identity,
  not a conclusion based only on the shared initial. The underlying civil register image remains
  a separate documentary check.
- **The Dublin trail currently ends at Henry Macartney, farmer**, named as Richard Henry's father
  in the earlier reading of the 1870 marriage register. The image could not be reopened here.
- **A separate Henry McCartney appears at Cromkill in a secondary 1861–62 valuation transcript.**
  No record identifies him as Richard's father; the broader four-candidate sweep remains unchecked.
- **The older Antrim names are records of people, not a proven pedigree.** Walter McCartny/Kels
  (1669) is reported by a specialist surname-history page citing Macafee entry 4176. I downloaded
  Macafee's 1740 PDF transcript and verified entries for McCartney “And” and John in Ahoghill;
  “And” is abbreviated, not a confirmed expansion. The 1796 PDF lists record 164, Samuel Sen.
  M’Cartney of Ballintoy, Cary, with one spinning wheel. Both are derivative lists, not original
  family records; neither names relationships to Henry, one another, or the Earl. A separate 1734
  Ballintoy census is a promising source, but its PDF/XLS could not be accessed and no McCartney
  entry was verified.
- **A 1772 Macartney estate map volume is a specific, actionable archive lead.** Ballymoney
  Ancestry says its tenant database transcribes names from PRONI D/1062/2/4/1. I confirmed the
  parent Macartney Papers reference at PRONI, but did not retrieve tenant names, establish which
  townlands the volume covers, or confirm that the manuscript is digitized. A tenant match alone
  would show tenancy, not kinship.
- **The Lissanoure/Earl line is separate.** The DNB supports the Earl as son of George Macartney
  of Lissanoure and Elizabeth Winder. No checked source joins that family to John’s Dublin line
  or to the Antrim tenant occurrences. The relationship remains unproved, not disproved.
- The 1901/1911 census API responses remain indexed household evidence rather than fresh readings
  of the scanned forms; the early Lissanoure pedigree remains a published genealogy, not a
  primary-record-verified chain.

### What the additional backward search found

- I directly read the **Irish Genealogy Hub transcription** of Connor’s 1861–62 Griffith’s Valuation. It lists **Henry, Hugh, James, John, William, and William Jr McCartney in Cromkill**, plus Arthur and Stafford McCartney in Slaght. This confirms the name/townland entries in that secondary transcription—not the original valuation image and not that this Henry is Richard’s father. [Connor valuation transcription](https://www.irishgenealogyhub.com/antrim/griffiths-valuation/parish-of-connor.php).
- I directly read the site’s **1835 Connor Tithe Applotment transcription**. It lists Mrs McCartney at Ballee and Mr, James, and John McCartney at Slatt; it does **not** list a Henry Macartney/McCartney. That is a useful time-and-place check, but a tithe-payer list is not a census and omission does not prove Henry was absent from the parish. [Connor tithe transcription](https://www.irishgenealogyhub.com/antrim/tithe-applotments/connor-parish.php).
- The **1851 Killead surviving-census-fragment transcription** lists several Macartney/McCartney households in Aughnamullan. The entries include Hugh Macartney, 56, farmer, and William Macartney, 80, farmer, with John, 40, farmer, described as his son; other entries include Hugh McCartney, 43, shoemaker. These people are not identified as Richard’s father and are in Killead, not evidence for the Connor candidate. [Killead 1851 fragments](https://www.cotyroneireland.com/census/killead.html).
- The IrishGenealogy record pages and 1870 marriage PDF returned **403**, so I could not inspect images directly. Details attributed to the 1870 and 1896 register images are earlier project human readings, not new image verification. No primary record found in this research connects Henry with Cromkill, Killead, Lissanoure, or the Earl.
- The pre-1800 leads remain unjoined. The 1669 Walter/Kels claim comes from a specialist secondary page citing Macafee entry 4176; the original hearth rolls were destroyed, and I did not inspect the surviving transcript. The downloaded 1740 transcription lists McCartney “And” and John in Ahoghill; “And” is not expanded as certain, and the original returns were destroyed. The downloaded 1796 derivative list gives Samuel Sen. M’Cartney in Ballintoy with one wheel; it gives parish and award, not townland or parentage. A separate 1734 Ballintoy religious census may provide a more local search because its source description says it lists townlands, but I could not access the PDF/XLS or verify any McCartney entry. **None of these sources links the names to Henry, to one another, or to the Lissanoure family.**
- The 1772 estate volume remains an archive lead, not a discovered ancestor: Ballymoney Ancestry says its tenant database is based on maps in PRONI D/1062/2/4/1, while PRONI’s index confirms the parent Macartney Papers collection D1062/2. I found no readable tenant rows or item-level map description; I did not establish whether Cromkill/Connor is covered or whether images are online. UHF describes separate 1705–1895 leases for Ballymoney town on the Earl of Antrim's estate (PRONI D2977/3A/3/2/10), which is a different landlord and cannot substitute for the Macartney volume. A second researcher independently confirmed those access limits; no tenant name from the 1772 volume is verified.

The practical stopping point is therefore: **John → Sheila/Kathleen (confirmed by John) → William Henry → Richard Henry → Henry Macartney (farmer)**. The next step earlier than Henry remains unresolved. Historical sources report separate Antrim occurrences in 1669, 1740, 1796, 1835 and 1861–62, but there is no connected genealogy from those occurrences to the Dublin father or to an ancestor in the Earl’s generation.

### 1a. Henry: only identified through an earlier register transcription

Earlier research transcribed Henry from one field of the 1870 St Mark's marriage register:
the father column, **"Henry Macartney, farmer"**. The register image was blocked in this pass,
so I did not independently reread that field. The earlier search reports no birth, marriage,
death or place record for him in the free indexes:

- his marriage predates civil registration (1845), so it is only in a church register;
- no baptism of any Richard Macartney/McCartney exists in the digitised church registers
  1825–55, so Richard's baptism is not in the free set;
- the will calendars contain **no probate or administration for any Henry Macartney** under
  Macartney, McCartney, McArtney or Mc Cartney, at any date;
- the civil death index 1864–1901 has five adult Henry candidates, but all five registers are
  handwritten and no reading has been banked. The 2026-10-09 memo lists their links.

This is not a contradiction to explain away. It is a missing record of exactly the generation
the comparison needs.

### 2a. Why the earlier memo ranked Cromkill highest (provisional)

Cromkill now has **direct transcription-level support**: the Connor 1861–62 valuation listing
names Henry, Hugh, James, John, William, and William Jr McCartney there. The earlier 1870
register transcription identifies Richard’s father as a farmer, but no record joins that father
to the Henry in the valuation. I did not inspect the original valuation page, revision books, or
Cromkill census forms in this pass. The earlier report’s later Matthew Macartney household
claim also remains unverified here. Occupation, locality, and surname clustering make Cromkill
worth testing; they do not establish identity or descent.

### 2b. Why it is still only a candidate

- **No record names Henry and Richard together.** The valuation gives a name, a townland and
  a holding — never a son in Dublin.
- The 1835 Connor Tithe Applotment transcription I read lists **Mrs McCartney (Ballee),
  Mr McCartney (Slatt), James McCartney (Slatt), and John McCartney (Slatt)**; it does not list
  Henry. It is a secondary transcription of tithe-payers, not a complete population register;
  absence from it does not prove that no Henry lived in the parish.
- The earlier memo says the Cromkill farmer Matthew does not name his father in either census
  and that a Henry appears in the Crappagh tithe books; neither claim was checked against the
  original records in this pass. Even if true, they would not connect a candidate to Richard.
- The earlier ranking retains Crappagh (Monaghan) as another possible candidate, but its
  valuation and tithe entries were not independently inspected here.

Provisional conclusion: the secondary valuation transcription places a Henry McCartney at
Cromkill in the right broad occupational region, while the earlier register transcription calls
Richard’s father a farmer. These facts make Cromkill worth testing, but the records have not
been joined. Keep Cromkill as a research lead, not a fact about Henry's residence.

### 3a. The 1772 Macartney estate volume: the most useful next archival test

[Ballymoney Ancestry's Macartney estate page](http://www.ballymoneyancestry.com/macartney-estate-records.aspx)
explicitly describes a database of tenants transcribed from a volume of maps drawn in 1772 and
gives the original PRONI reference as **D/1062/2/4/1**. Its account says the Macartney family
bought an estate in Loughguile parish in 1733 and later acquired additional townlands.
[PRONI's Significant Private Deposits index](https://www.proni.gov.uk/publications/html-document/index-significant-private-deposits)
independently lists the parent **Macartney Papers, D1062/2**. These sources establish a credible finding aid and archival route; they do
**not** establish that the 1772 volume covers Cromkill, that a McCartney tenant appears in it,
or that any tenant was related to the owners.

I could not retrieve an item-level catalogue description or manuscript images for D/1062/2/4/1.
PRONI says its eCatalogue's “View” option appears only for digitally available records, and that
most records require an in-person visit. The Ulster Historical Foundation describes Ballymoney
Ancestry as a free searchable database of local records, but the accessible page for this item
describes the database without exposing its tenant rows. UHF's separate tenant database describes
leases for Ballymoney town on the Earl of Antrim's estate, 1705–1895 (PRONI D2977/3A/3/2/10).
That is a different landlord's estate, and no evidence shows it contains Macartney tenants; it
must not be conflated with D1062/2. I did not locate an indexed tenant entry for the Macartney
volume, so no candidate name is banked here.

The best focused question to PRONI is: **Does D1062/2/4/1 cover Cromkill or any Connor-parish
townland, and is the volume or a tenant index available to view or copy?** PRONI's general
archive advice is free; its paid Search Service is restricted to specific information in clearly
identified archives and does not undertake speculative searches for every occurrence of a
surname. A copy request can cite the exact reference, but PRONI does not search as part of a copy
order. PRONI also states that standard Search Room visits are free and do not require an
appointment. [PRONI catalogue](https://www.proni.gov.uk/proni-ecatalogue) · [PRONI Search
Service](https://www.proni.gov.uk/proni-search-service) · [PRONI visit information](https://www.proni.gov.uk/your-visit).

Even if a tenant list yields a McCartney in the district, it would establish a landholding or
tenancy only. To bridge the 1772 person to Henry (father of Richard) would still require records
naming relationships across the intervening generations. None has been found in this pass.

## 6. Why no link is established — gaps in the evidence

1. **The Earl's immediate line did not continue through children, according to the sources
   read.** The DNB calls him the only son of George Macartney of Lissanoure; the Glens account
   says he had no children and describes estate succession through a niece/sister's daughter.
   The accounts differ in the heir's naming/relationship detail, so verify against wills and
   pedigrees. A proposed relationship to the Dublin family would need evidence for a cadet
   branch or shared earlier ancestor, not assumed descent from the Earl.
2. **The documented line is gentry, and no farmer cadet is recorded.** From 1626 to 1943 every
   generation in the pedigrees is an officer, clergyman, magistrate, MP or landowner. The one
   cadet branch named (Arthur, Belfast) is recorded only into the 1720s.
3. **The earlier research identifies Henry only through a transcribed father field.** The
   1870 register image and the prior index searches were not accessible in this pass. His
   parentage and residence remain unverified here; absence from searched indexes would not
   prove that no other record exists.
4. **A Henry McCartney is listed at Cromkill, but is not tied to Richard.** This pass directly
   read a secondary transcription of that valuation entry; it did not inspect the original image
   or independently verify the earlier four-candidate count across Ireland.
5. **Geographic proximity would not prove kinship.** Lissanoure and the transcribed Cromkill
   Henry are both in Antrim, but no record places Richard’s father at Cromkill or connects that
   tenant to Lissanoure. A county/parish match plus a shared surname remains insufficient to
   establish descent.
6. **The likely period is difficult, but the losses need record-by-record qualification.**
   The 1922 Four Courts fire destroyed many Irish records, but not every will, register, or
   copy. Civil registration began in 1864; the earlier report says relevant censuses/registers
   are absent from free online coverage. That is a research obstacle, not proof the records
   never existed.
7. **The surname is not an identifier.** Macartney, McCartney, Mc Cartney and M'Cartney
   occur in multiple families. A same-name return in a nearby parish would be a lead only;
   identity and descent need a chain of records.

   candidate holding over time; they cannot by themselves link a tenant to Richard in Dublin.
7. **Read the five candidate Henry death registers, 1864–1901.** Handwritten occupation and
   place-of-death columns may distinguish a farmer from other namesakes; use a human read, not
   OCR.
8. **Inspect the 1772 Macartney estate map volume, PRONI D/1062/2/4/1.** Ballymoney Ancestry
   says its tenant database transcribes this volume; PRONI's index confirms the parent Macartney
   Papers collection D1062/2. First establish whether the maps cover Cromkill/Connor and whether
   a searchable tenant index or digital images exist. PRONI offers free general archive advice;
   its paid Search Service is for specific queries in identified archives and excludes speculative
   surname sweeps. A focused question is: “Does D1062/2/4/1 cover Cromkill or any Connor-parish
   townland, and is the volume or tenant index available to view or copy?” A tenant match would
   establish tenancy only, not descent.
9. **Check PRONI Griffith revisions, VAL/12/B, for Cromkill** and identify successors to the
   1861 occupiers. PRONI says the digitised series runs 1864–1933; a changed occupier proves
   tenure succession, not parentage. Search Cromkill and spelling variants directly.
10. **Use the Antrim 1669/1740/1796 leads as a separate name-distribution lane.** Request/inspect
   the relevant PRONI transcripts (T/307 for 1669; T808/15258 for 1740) and the original 1796
   flax list, then look for people explicitly linked to later records. Do not join generations by
   matching names or location alone.
10. **Consider Y-DNA only if suitable testers and documented comparison lines exist.** A
   mismatch may exclude a shared direct male line; a match alone would not identify a specific
   ancestor or date the connection.

## 11. Application implementation note (not genealogical evidence)

This section records how the site presents the research; it is not historical evidence.

Henry has an optional, typed `researchEvidence` value directly on the `Person` record
shape. `src/lib/seed-data.ts` builds that field from the canonical `macartney-lines.ts`
records, the seed persists it in `people.research_evidence` (JSONB), and DB reads hydrate it.
Both his profile and `/compare` render the record's attached value. This is evidence metadata,
not a family edge; no parent was added. The tree is sourced from `seedPeople()` and persisted
into Postgres `people` rows (with JSONB evidence); reads hydrate the stored value. A guarded
compatibility path recognizes the original Henry seed row (stable id 13 plus name/cluster check)
