# Henry Macartney's place — the venue sweep and the junction verdict, 2026-10-09

Follow-up to `mabel-and-parents-2026-10-07.md`. The plan's load-bearing gap is
now this one: **Henry Macartney, "farmer" in the father column of the 1870 St
Mark's marriage register, has no place of any kind**, and the junction test —
is our Dublin line connected to the documented Macartneys of Lissanoure? —
cannot start until he gets one.

This pass worked backwards from every record we hold and forwards from the
documented line's cadets. Every new claim is banked as a sourced fact on the
live site (family findings on person 28, the Lissanoure sweep on cluster
person 37, both in the correct cluster — never merged). Facts 99–106.

## 1. Backwards: what the free indexes can and cannot do

Searches run 9 Oct 2026 against irishgenealogy.ie (GRO civil indexes and church
records) and the NAI Calendar of Wills and Administrations (free search hosted
by PRONI). All spellings tried: Macartney, McCartney, McArtney, MacCartney,
McCartny, Macartny.

- **Civil deaths**, Henry × every spelling, 1864–1901: five adult candidates,
  twenty-plus child rows. MacCartney/McCartny/Macartny: none at all.
- **Civil marriages**, Henry × every spelling, 1845–1875: four hits, and all
  four are *later* than the birth of his son Richard Henry (~1841) — so none is
  his own marriage. **He married before civil registration (1845)**; his
  marriage belongs in the church registers.
- **Church records**: no baptism of any Richard Macartney or McCartney anywhere
  in Ireland 1825–1855 — his baptism is not in the digitised registers. The
  index's "parent = Henry Macartney" filter is **not trustworthy**: the first
  hit checked (William Macartney, St George's, Dublin, 12 Aug 1838, 13 Portland
  Street) has father **James**, mother Maria. It appears to match stray text,
  not the parent field. Do not read the 36-row result as "families with a
  father Henry".
- **Wills and administrations** (the one source here that gives an address and
  an occupation in one line): **no Henry Macartney ever took probate or
  administration, at any date.** The only entries with Henry in the name are
  James Henry Macartney (d. 16 Nov 1887) and Carlile Henry Hayes Macartney
  (d. 1954, Surrey). McCartney/McArtney × Henry 1864–1905: nothing.

## 2. Where the surname's farmers actually were

The will calendars are the first source this pass that puts **farmers named
Macartney on named townlands**, and the pattern is clean: 1864–1905, **every
Macartney farmer entry sits in Counties Antrim or Down** —

- **Antrim**: Derriaghy (Alice, 1904), Ballydonaghy (Andrew, d. 9 Jan 1892),
  Aughnamillan (Hugh, d. 1 May 1888; William John, d. 13 Jan 1893),
  Boltnaconnell (Thomas, 1905);
- **Down**: Cabra (David, d. 1 Jan 1891), Granshaw (Hans Gray, d. 16 Apr 1889),
  Ballyhanwood (Hugh Senior, d. 6 Dec 1879), Lisnod (John, d. 28 Dec 1893),
  Ballymacreely (John, d. 11 Feb 1892), Ballyailley (John, d. 19 Jan 1875),
  Ballylesson (Thomas, d. 1898, retired).

**No Henry among them, and not one in Dublin.** Under the McCartney spelling
the farmer layer widens across Ulster (Londonderry, Tyrone, Armagh, Monaghan,
one Louth). So the honest shape of the problem: our farmer's surname belongs to
Ulster's farming population — which is exactly why same-surname reasoning is
worthless here — yet no probate record ties any of those townlands to him.

## 3. Forwards: the Lissanoure line's cadets

Swept against Lodge/Burke's as transcribed by Stirnet (`Macartney1`,
`Macartney2`) plus Burke's 1879 on archive.org and the Glens of Antrim history:

- The Sheriff's **elder son James** (by Jane Calderwood) was a Judge of the
  Court of Common Pleas; his line survived only in grand-daughters (Frances
  m. 1748 Fulke Greville; Mary m. 1761 William Henry Lyttelton). No male line.
- By his second wife: **Chichester (dsp)** and **George of Lissanoure** — the
  estate line, gentry throughout, ending with George Travers Lucy (1943).
- A **separate documented family** carried the name in the north:
  *Macartney of Blacket/Belfast/Antrim* — Isaac Macartney, High Sheriff of co.
  Antrim 1690; the Rev. W. G. Macartney of Killead died s.p. 1858 and the
  property passed to a nephew who took the name **Ellison-Macartney** (Clogher
  Park, co. Tyrone). And the baronets **Macartney of Lish**, co. Armagh (Sir
  William Isaac, 2nd Bt, rector of Desertogny; Hussey Burgh Macartney, Dean of
  Melbourne, d. 1894), with a Donegal cadet (George Augustus Chichester
  Macartney of Glendarragh).
- The Glens history names only one of the Sheriff's "four sons" (George of
  Lissanoure). Burke's gives the family seats as "Lissanoure, Ballymoney, and
  **Lowther Lodge, Balbriggan, co. Dublin**" — Lowther Lodge coming from Ellen
  Filgate's inheritance in 1828. That is the line's only documented Dublin
  property and the only place the two stories touch a Dublin address; it is
  recorded, not claimed.

**No documented branch is a farmer, and no Henry Macartney appears in any of
them.** Across the entire will-calendar database (1858 onward), the surname has
no probate entry with the forename Henry at all.

## 4. The junction verdict

**Same surname, no provable link — the prior stands, and no record moved it.**

What would have to be true for the connection to hold: a documented branch
would have to have shed an *undocumented* younger son who became a tenant
farmer c.1800–1820, whose son Richard Henry somehow reached Dublin by 1870,
married by licence at St Mark's, and whose own son left no trace of the
family's origin. That is entirely possible — younger sons vanish from pedigrees
all the time — but **nothing in any record supports it**, and the two named
traps stay named: a tenant called Macartney on a Macartney estate proves
nothing, and the northern Macartney farmer townlands (§2) are a pool, not a
match.

## 5. Could not be run (blocked, not skipped)

- **Griffith's Valuation / Tithe Applotment Books** — the free Griffith's
  search (askaboutireland.ie) is behind a Cloudflare bot challenge that rejects
  every automated request (curl and a real Chrome over CDP both get "Just a
  moment…"), and the Tithe Applotment site no longer resolves. **These are the
  single best sources for putting a farmer on a townland, and they need a
  human browser session** — John, five minutes: askaboutireland.ie → Griffith's
  Valuation → surname Macartney/McCartney, County Antrim then Down.
- **1901/1911 census** (census.nationalarchives.ie) — still unreachable from
  this machine (45 s timeouts), the same failure as 7 Oct.
- **The 1870 Dublin diocesan marriage-licence bond** — not online anywhere
  free; it survives in the RCBL / National Archives (Dublin diocese). It is
  the record that names the groom's parish, so it is the next hard target.
- **Death-register occupation/place columns** — reachable (links in §1) but
  handwritten; they need eyes, not OCR.
- **The in-app browser panel** would not attach, so the playtest of the new
  facts was done over HTTP as the signed-in user (see the report).

## 6. Next testable step, in order

1. **Human read of the five candidate death registers** (links in §1): the
   occupation column separates farmer from labourer at a glance, and the
   place-of-death column gives the district's townland. One hit puts Henry on
   a map; five misses is a real negative.
2. **Griffith's Valuation under every spelling** in Antrim and Down (human
   browser, see §5) — first source to list a Henry Macartney *holding land*.
3. **Dublin diocesan marriage-licence bond, 1870** (RCBL/NAI) — names the
   groom's parish; the cleanest single key to our side's place.
4. **1901/1911 census** for Macartney farming households in the §2 townlands
   (retry when NAI's census site answers) — turns the will-calendar townlands
   into households with ages.
5. Only then the junction: a *documented* descent from any Lissanoure cadet to
   a farmer named Henry. Until that exists, the prior holds.
