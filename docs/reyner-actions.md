## C. Elizabeth ("Betty") — working order

**Identification basis:** Dorothy Elizabeth Reyner, b. Dec 1915 (10 y 4 m at the 18 Apr 1926 census), Church of Ireland, 32 Carnew Street. The family's "Betty" is taken to be her because she is the only Elizabeth among the four daughters, and "Dorothy Elizabeth → Betty" is the ordinary Irish contraction. **High confidence, not proof** — the marriage certificate in C1 closes it, because it names her father.

**The admission date matters more than the death date.** Mary Jane may have been in Grangegorman for years before July 1946 — asylums of that era often held long-stay patients. If so, the household changed at her **admission**, not at her death, and Betty took over the house and the raising of John Mervyn from that date rather than from 1946. Ask for the admission date explicitly and treat it as the pivot of your father's childhood.

1. **Her marriage certificate** — the unlock. Civil marriages, surname **Reyner** (try **Reynor**), first name **Dorothy**, 1936–1965. Gives: her address at marriage, her husband's name and occupation, and her father's name and trade. Remember the civil index **strips middle names**, so she is indexed as plain *Dorothy Reyner*. She was 30 when her mother died, so a marriage **before** 1946 is more likely than after — and if she married before, the family remembered her as a married sister, not an unmarried one.
2. **The unmarried branch — test it first, it is cheaper.** Civil **deaths**, surname **Reyner**, first name **Dorothy**, 1950–1980. The surname is rare enough that a hit is almost certainly her, and one hit would settle that she never married, which in turn makes the electoral registers and the family plot the two ways in. The family calling her "Betty" rather than a married name is a faint hint this is the right branch.
2. **The 1939 Register** (England & Wales, 29 Sept 1939) — she would be 23. FindMyPast. If she had already gone across, this finds her and gives her occupation and household.
3. **Dublin electoral registers, 1946–1955** — free at Dublin City Library & Archive, Pearse Street, and at the NAI. Check **32 Carnew Road** year by year: if the Reyners are still there, the house did not break up. If Betty is registered as *Reyner*, she kept house for her brother and never married before then.
4. **Her siblings' marriage certificates** — Arthur Noel (b. 1912) and Helen Isobel (b. 1914). Each gives the family address at that date, and Betty very often appears among the **witnesses**. Witness names are not indexed, so this is a read-the-certificate job, not a search.
5. **Her death** — only reachable once a married name is known, so it waits behind 1–2.

**Fallback if 1 and 2 both fail:** she may have married in England after 1939, or never married at all. In that case the electoral register (3) and the cemetery registers (B) are the only routes, and the asylum next-of-kin field (A1) becomes the fastest way to place her.

### H7a. Why this identification is sound

Unlike the Grangegorman entry, the informant here is **not** an official or a fellow patient — it is his own daughter, giving her relationship and her address. That is the strongest class of informant in the register.

- **Eva Constance** is the two-year-old *Eva* of the 1901 household (Bow Bridge/Camac View), twelve and still at home at Camac Terrace in 1911. The same person, named in full, describing herself as his daughter.
- **Railway Pensioner** matches a man who was Head Porter, Railway Servant (1901) and Railway Servant (1911) — a full career, ended, and pensioned.
- **Married**, not widowed, in 1938 — consistent with Hannah (b. c.1873, so 65 in 1938) being alive but long since living apart (H6).

### H7b. What it settles and what it opens

**Settles the collision in H4/H5.** Joseph died in 1938 in Dún Laoghaire, with his daughter present. The Grangegorman *Samuel Reyner*, age 73, of Leland Place, therefore cannot be him — so that entry is **Samuel Reyner of Blythe's Avenue, railway checker, born 1858/59**, a probable brother. The 1932 identification now rests on age, address *and* elimination rather than on age and address alone.

**And it retires the name "Samuel Joseph".** The great-grandfather was recorded as **Joseph** in 1901, in 1911 and at his death. The only reason his first name was ever written *Samuel* was the need to reconcile him with an official record that called the 1932 decedent *Samuel* — and that record is now someone else. **Drop it: he was Joseph Reyner.**

**Opens the end of Joseph's life.** He died in the **Dún Laoghaire** registration district, not Drumcondra, and his daughter was living at **45 Glasthule Road** — a few hundred yards away. The obvious reading is that he spent his last years in or near his daughter's household, not in the old Carnew Street/Camac Terrace area. He was **not** in Samuel Arthur's 32 Carnew Street household in April 1926 (seven people, none of them him), so he had left that household at least twelve years before he died. **Finding him in the 1926 census is now the single most informative free step** — it would show where a 65-year-old railway pensioner lived while his son's family had the old house.

**St John's Parochial Hall needs explaining.** A parochial hall is not a house. Two readings, and they are not exclusive: (a) it was in use as an auxiliary hospital, nursing home or temporary infirmary, and he died there; or (b) the registrar entered the parish/registration place rather than the exact dwelling. Worth one line in the Grangegorman-style letter — if he died in an institution, there may be a record of it. Do not assume either.

**Feeds the railway archive directly.** "Railway Pensioner" plus an exact death date is the minimum a staff record needs: the **Irish Railway Record Society** staff records (section D) should now be asked for *Joseph Reyner, railway servant/porter, pensioned, died 14 Dec 1938*, as well as for Samuel Arthur.

**The Phillips line is now open and free to name.** Eva Constance Reyner married a **Phillips**; her marriage is in the civil index between 1911 and 1938 under bride *Reyner*, and will name her father as Joseph Reyner, railway official — an independent confirmation of the father–daughter link rather than a new fact. Low priority; do it if the index is already open.

**A coastal echo, offered as nothing more than that.** 45 Glasthule Road sits between Blackrock and Dalkey. The family memory of "Black Rock" has been attributed to the user's mother and parked. This is the first **documented** coastal address anywhere in the Reyner line — 1938, and in Eva's generation, not the direct line. It does not explain the memory; it is the only Reyner footprint on that coast so far, and it is worth remembering if the Blackrock question is ever reopened.

## H8. John Mervyn's birth, 1932 — why irishgenealogy.ie returns nothing

**The coverage, checked 2026-10-27 (Citizens Information, edited 11 July 2025; the site header says births to 1925):**

| Register | Free range on irishgenealogy.ie |
|---|---|
| Births | **1864–1924/25** |
| Marriages | 1845–1949/50 |
| Deaths | **1871–1974/75** |

So the three entries found in this line so far — Sammy 1933, Joseph 1938, Mary Jane 1946 — are **deaths**, and deaths run into the 1970s. A **1932 birth is seven or eight years past the edge** of the birth range, and the site extends that range roughly a year at a time. **The blank result is a fact about coverage, not about the name and not about the searching.** Do not re-run it; wait until the birth range reaches 1932, or use the routes below.

### The index that does cover 1932

- **FamilySearch, *Ireland, Civil Registration Indexes, 1845–1958*** — free account, **index only** (no images), excludes the six counties. This is the direct replacement for the blank irishgenealogy search, and it gives **registration district, volume and page**, which is exactly what an order needs.
- **FindMyPast, Irish birth index** — same content, subscribed.

**Search mechanics, learned the hard way on this surname:**
- **Surname only, no forename.** The civil index **strips middle names**, so *John Mervyn* may be indexed as plain **John**, or as **Mervyn** if that was the first name registered and *John* the second. A forename in the search box is how this goes wrong.
- **All variants:** Reyner, Reynor, Rayner, Raynor, Ryner, Reiner, Rainer. The family's own spelling was unstable through the 1920s and 1930s — the 1926 census page reads closer to *Reynor*.
- **The index carries no mother's maiden name** (unlike England & Wales after 1911), so the entry cannot be filtered by *Evans*. Timing and district are the only filters.
- **Districts to check:** **Dublin North** (32 Carnew Street is north city, off the North Circular Road — the default expectation); **Dublin South**; and **Rathdown**, which covers Blackrock, Kingstown/Dún Laoghaire and north Wicklow. If Mary Jane was lying in with her own Evans people at Greystones in 1932, the birth is registered at Rathdown, not Dublin.

### Then order it

With year + district + volume + page: **€20** full certified certificate online via the HSE; or **€5** uncertified research photocopy from the **GRO Research Room, Werburgh Street, Dublin 2** — walk-in **Tuesdays only** (9.30–12.30, 14.00–16.30), email service five days a week at `GROResearchRoom@welfare.ie`. The Research Room indexes cover the Republic **from 1922 onwards**, so the 1932 entry can be found there in person even with no online index at all.

**What the certificate is worth.** For a 1932 birth the informant is normally a parent, so column by column it gives: exact date, place of birth (a street address — the family's address in 1932, which confirms rather than discovers 32 Carnew Street, already documented in 1926 and 1933), father's occupation (Samuel Arthur, passenger guard), mother's maiden name (**Evans**), and the informant's signature. Modest genealogical yield, but it is the only record that **starts the chain with a document** instead of a family recollection — everything above his father currently begins with a 1932 date remembered, not recorded.

### Two cheaper parallel routes

1. **UK naturalisation file** (The National Archives, Kew — **HO 334**, certificates of naturalisation, 1960s–70s; search Discovery by name, free). A certificate of naturalisation states **date and place of birth**. It therefore answers the birth question *and* the "when did he become a UK citizen" question that is currently only "the 60s or 70s" — two gaps for one file. **This is the best-value item in H8.**
2. **Baptism.** If the family was Church of Ireland, Dublin parish registers are held at the **RCB Library, Braemor Park, Churchtown** — post-1900 registers are not online anywhere, so this is an archive enquiry, not a search. A baptism entry gives the date of birth and both parents' names. A 1932 Dublin baptism may also name the address.

### The Accra marriage, not yet used

His marriage to **Margaret Davies**, Accra, c.1956–57 (Gold Coast, just before independence in March 1957) — a marriage certificate states his **age** and his **father's name and occupation**. That is an independent check on a 1932 birth and a second witness to Samuel Arthur at a date after the 1926 census. Records sit with Ghana's **Register-General's Department**, or with the officiating church (mission registers); harder to reach than the Irish ones, but it is the only document that speaks about him between 1946 and the 1960s.
