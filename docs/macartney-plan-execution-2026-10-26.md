# Executing the plan — Henry Macartney of Cromkill, 2026-10-26

This is the report the 26 October plan was meant to produce. It records what
each gate returned, what was verified against a primary record rather than a
transcription, what failed because the source was unreachable, and the two
records that would now settle the question.

**The short version.** The Cromkill candidate is no longer a transcription
claim. I read the original valuation revision book and it lists **six McCartney
tenements in Cromkill**, including **Henry McCartney holding a 27-acre farm
directly of the Representatives of George Gray**. The family there was
Presbyterian and still farming the townland in 1911. Separately, PRONI's church
guide shows that exactly **one** register survives for a c.1841 baptism in that
parish: **Connor Presbyterian, baptisms 1819–1963, MIC1P/162**. That is now the
single most valuable record in this research, and it is a two-hour job at PRONI
or a subscription lookup, not a research problem.

The link is still **unproved**. Nothing found today names Henry of Cromkill as
Richard's father. What changed is that the candidate is now evidenced from the
original record, the family's religion and continuity are established, and the
one register that could name Richard as Henry's son has been identified with its
exact reference.

## 2. Gate 4 — the land evidence, from the original record

### 2a. The whole-island sweep (transcriptions)

I crawled the parish-level Griffith's and tithe transcriptions for the six
counties that site covers (Antrim, Down, Armagh, Tyrone, Cavan, Donegal):
**228 Macartney/McCartney occupier rows in Griffith's, 96 in the tithe books.**

- **Exactly one Henry appears in the entire Griffith's sweep**: `McCartney,
  Henry — Cromkill`, listed **twice** (two tenements). Connor parish, Antrim.
- The 1835 Connor tithe applotment lists **no Henry at all**: `McCartney, Mrs.`
  at Ballee, and `Mr.`, `James`, `John` at Slatt (Slaght). So the McCartneys
  were in the neighbouring townlands in 1835 and at Cromkill by 1861.
- Londonderry, Monaghan, Louth, Meath and Dublin are **not covered** by that
  transcription site, so the sweep cannot support "no Henry anywhere else in
  Ireland". It supports: in the six counties swept, one Henry, at Cromkill.

### 2b. The original book — VAL/12/B/3/18A, 1863–1880

PRONI's valuation revision books are reachable and free. The townland index of
volume VAL/12/B/3/18A (Union of Ballymena, Electoral Division of Kells) puts
Cromkill at page 22; the entries run across folios 52–73. The McCartney rows,
read from the images:

| Ref | Occupier | Immediate lessor | Tenement | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25b | **Henry McCartney** | Alex Bell | House | a house only |
| 28a | John McCartney | (Gray) | House and land | |
| 28b | Hugh McCartney | John McCartney | House | sub-tenancy |
| 28c | William McCartney | John McCartney | House | sub-tenancy |
| 29a | **Henry McCartney** | **Reps. of George Gray** | House and land, c. 27 acres, land £22 5s, total £24 | the principal farm |
| 29b | John Greenwood | Henry McCartney | House and garden | Henry as middleman |
| 29c | David Johnston | Henry McCartney | House and garden | Henry as middleman |
| 30a | James McCartney | (Gray) | House and land | |

This matters for three reasons.

1. **It corrects the earlier uncertainty.** The six Cromkills were a
   transcription claim; they are now read from the revision book itself.
2. **It names the landlord**: the *Representatives of George Gray*, not a
   Macartney. Any estate-paper search now has a target, and the Loughguile /
   Earl-of-Antrim hypothesis has no support at Cromkill at all.
3. **It shows a ranked family, not six equals.** Henry holds the 27-acre farm
   directly of the landlord and sublets two houses and gardens to Greenwood and
   Johnston. That is the profile of a farmer of standing in the townland — the
   kind of man whose son could be sent to Dublin and described in 1870 as a
   "Commissariat Assistant".

### 2c. The family in 1901 and 1911

From the National Archives census API (index data, not yet image-checked):

- **1901, house 14**: Matthew Macartney, 55, **farmer**; Ann, 44, wife;
  children Thomas 21 (national school teacher), Mary E 17, William 15,
  Sarah 11, Matthew M 9, Margretta 7, Thompson 5, Isabella 3.
- **1911, house 16**: Matthew, 65, farmer; Anne, 55; Sarah 21 (N S teacher),
  Matthew M 20, Margaretta 18, Thompson 17, Isabella 15.
- **James McCartney**, 70, linen weaver in 1901 (house 65); 80, **farmer** in
  1911 (house 39), with Ellen, 63.
- **Ann McCartney**, 80, head of household in 1901 (house 12).

Every one of them is recorded **Presbyterian**, born Co. Antrim. A McCartney
family of farmers held ground at Cromkill continuously from at least 1861 to
1911 — and the townland is the most McCartney-dense in Ireland in the Irish
Roots dataset (6 Griffith's entries, 29 census records; next is Slaght with 3
and 12).

### 2d. Why this is a candidate and not an identification

Matthew (b. c.1846) is close enough in age to be Richard's brother; James
(b. c.1831) could be another. But **no record read today places Richard in
Cromkill, or names Henry of Cromkill as anyone's father.** The 1870 register
gives the father's name and occupation only — no residence, no age, no
"deceased" note, which suggests Henry was alive in 1870 and therefore is on the
1863–1880 revision pages, exactly where he appears.

## 3. Gate 3 — where the baptism actually is

From *PRONI Guide to Church Records* (2019), p. 164, entry **CONNOR, CO.
ANTRIM**:

- **C.I. Connor (Connor diocese)** — *"Earliest records destroyed in Dublin"*:
  baptisms from **1871**, marriages 1845–, burials 1879–.
- **P. Connor** — **baptisms 1819–1963**, marriages 1845–1900, **MIC1P/162**.
- **P. Kells** — baptisms **1873–97**, marriages 1889–1921, **MIC1P/310**.

Read together with the fact that the Cromkill McCartneys were Presbyterian,
this is decisive for method: **a Richard Macartney baptised c.1841 in Connor
parish can only be in MIC1P/162.** There is nowhere else for it to be. The
earlier conclusion that "no baptism exists" was true only of the digitised
Church of Ireland and Catholic sets — and in this parish the Church of Ireland
register does not begin until 1871 anyway.

The same reel should also yield Henry's other children, which is what would
turn a name into a family: siblings of Richard baptised 1819–1863 to a Henry
Macartney of Cromkill.

## 5. The verdict, restated

1. **The line is documented to Henry Macartney, farmer, and no further.** That
   still rests on the father field of the 1870 St Mark's register.
2. **Cromkill is the best-evidenced candidate for his farm**, on four
   independent grounds: the only Henry in a six-county Griffith's sweep; six
   McCartney tenements in the original revision book; a 27-acre farm held
   directly of the landlord; and a Presbyterian farming family of the same name
   still there in 1911.
3. **The Earl connection is not supported.** Cromkill was Gray-estate land. The
   Loughguile Macartneys were a different landlord's family in a different
   barony. Nothing read in two research passes joins them.
4. **What would prove it**, in order: (a) MIC1P/162 for a Richard, son of
   Henry, c.1841, with siblings; (b) Henry's death 1864–1884, Ballymena
   district, with a son as informant; (c) a marriage 1864+ of Matthew or James
   Macartney of Cromkill naming Henry as father. Any one of the three would
   join a generation; (a) would also fix the townland.

## 6. Honesty ledger

- "Read directly" means the image or API response was inspected in this pass.
  The revision-book entries in §2b are from the PRONI images; the valuation
  figures are as legible on those images and the acreage is stated as
  approximately 27 acres because the area columns are cramped.
- The 1901/1911 households are **index data**, not census-form images. Ages are
  as transcribed by the National Archives; the 1911 ages are one year more
  consistent than usual because both returns were taken in April.
- Matthew and James are **not** asserted to be Richard's brothers. Their ages
  make it possible; nothing read makes it likely.
- The Griffith's/tithe sweeps are transcriptions of transcriptions and cover
  six counties only. Absence outside those six is not evidence.
- Still unresolved from earlier passes: the 1734 Ballintoy census, the 1772
  Macartney estate map volume, the 1669/1740/1796 surname occurrences, and the
  five unread Henry deaths. None of them was advanced today.
- No DNA was ordered and no one was contacted. Both need the owner's decision.
